# **TASK 1 — Social Engineering**

### **1. What is Social Engineering?**

**Social Engineering** is a cyberattack in which a human is targeted instead of a computer. It is also known as **People Hacking**.

- Attackers trick people into revealing sensitive information or providing unauthorized access.
- Example: Asking a victim for their password instead of guessing or brute-forcing it.
- Attacks can be multi-layered and escalate through the **Snowball Effect**.

### **2. Snowball Effect**

Attackers collect small amounts of information and gradually use them to obtain more sensitive information.

**Example:**

Public information from Social Media → Phone/Broadband Provider Information → More Personal Information → Bank Account Access.

### **3. Other Forms of Social Engineering**

- **Malicious USB:** Leaving infected USB drives in public places, hoping employees will plug them into work computers.
- **Malicious Charging Cable:** Leaving cables containing malware, keyloggers or device-control tools in public places.
- **Direct Interaction:** Tricking people through phone calls or messages to obtain sensitive information.

### **4. Case Study — Stuxnet**

- **Stuxnet** was a computer virus (worm) originally used to target the **Iran nuclear programme in 2009**.
- It was allegedly developed by the governments of the United States and Israel.
- It could self-replicate across networks, including the internet.
- It spread beyond its intended target, and multiple variants emerged.

**Original Infection Method:**

1. Attackers dropped malicious USB devices in places where workers could find them.
2. Employees of companies connected to the facility found and plugged in the USB devices.
3. The malware entered the isolated network.
4. Stuxnet caused severe damage to Iran's nuclear programme and destroyed many nuclear centrifuges.

**Key Point:** Social engineering can exploit human curiosity and behaviour to bypass even isolated network security.

### **5. Staying Safe from Social Engineering Attacks**

1. **Multiple Authentication:** Set up multiple forms of authentication and use difficult-to-guess security question answers. Store answers securely.
2. **Avoid Unknown External Media:** Never plug unknown USBs, CDs or other external media into your computer.
3. **Verify Identity:** Always ask for proof of identity when strangers contact you claiming to represent a company.
4. **Confirm Communication:** Contact the company using a trusted phone number or email address.
5. **Never Share Passwords:** Legitimate employees will never ask for your password or account-protection information.

# **TASK 2 — Social Engineering: Phishing**

### **1. Overview**

- **Phishing** is one of the most common cyberattacks used by scammers and malicious attackers.
- Targets both individuals and businesses.
- Often acts as an **Initial Attack Vector** to gain access to company infrastructure before launching further attacks against corporate networks.
- Despite automated security tools, phishing remains one of the most common attack methods.

### **2. What is Phishing?**

**Phishing** is a type of Social Engineering attack in which an attacker tricks a victim into opening a malicious webpage through emails, text messages or other online communication.

**Types of Phishing Communication:**

| Type               | Meaning                      |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| **Email Phishing** | Phishing through emails      |
| **Smishing**       | Phishing through SMS         |
| **Vishing**        | Phishing through voice calls |

- Phishing traditionally referred to email-based attacks.
- It now includes instant messaging, SMS and voice/video communication.
- Attackers often use leaked or stolen email addresses and phone numbers to conduct large-scale campaigns.

### **3. How Does Phishing Work?**

Phishing messages use **Psychological Trickery**, such as creating a false sense of urgency, to make victims act quickly without thinking.

**Attack Process:**

1. Attacker sends a message containing a malicious link.
2. Victim clicks the link and opens an attacker-controlled webpage.
3. Victim enters sensitive information such as login credentials or credit card details.
4. The malicious website stores or sends the information to the attacker.
5. Attacker uses the stolen information to access the victim's account.

ALTERNATIVE ATTACK

The victim may also unknowingly install malware from the malicious webpage, giving the attacker an entry point into their device and network.

### **4. Types of Phishing Attacks**

| Attack Type          | Description                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **General Phishing** | Mass phishing targeting large groups without targeting specific individuals. Usually less sophisticated and may contain visible errors.              |
| **Spearphishing**    | Targeted phishing aimed at a specific individual or small group, such as employees of a company. Messages are more carefully crafted.                |
| **Whaling**          | Highly targeted phishing aimed at high-value individuals, such as C-suite executives. Messages are extremely well-crafted and difficult to identify. |

**Remember the difference:**

- General Phishing → Large groups
- Spearphishing → Specific individuals/groups
- Whaling → High-value executives

General phishing is more commonly encountered in daily life, whereas spearphishing and whaling may be more relevant in professional environments.

### **5. Real-World Example — Amazon Phishing Attack**

**Scenario:** An attacker sends an email pretending to be Amazon, claiming that an expensive item has been purchased from the victim's account.

<img width="1082" height="744" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/9cca27b9-faaf-4fbb-9892-9d9c72006d86" />

**Attack Process:**

1. Attacker sends a mass phishing email campaign.
2. Victims receive the email and some click the malicious link.
3. Victims enter their credentials on a fake Amazon login page.
4. The fake webpage stores or sends credentials to the attacker.
5. Attacker uses the stolen credentials to access the real Amazon account and purchase expensive items.

**Important:** The fake website may look identical to the original Amazon website. Victims may even be redirected to the real order-history page after submitting their credentials.

**Key Point:** Attackers often clone existing websites using freely available tools to make fake webpages appear genuine.

### **6. Identifying Phishing Attacks**

Common signs of phishing include:

- **Poor Grammar:** Spelling and grammatical mistakes.
- **Generic Greetings:** Messages such as "Dear Customer" instead of addressing the victim by name.
- **False Urgency:** Messages pressuring victims to act immediately.
- **Suspicious Domain Names:** Domains that look similar but are not identical to legitimate websites.
- **Suspicious Sender Address:** Emails sent from unrelated or free email accounts, such as Gmail, while pretending to represent a company.

**Example — Domain Spoofing (2021)**

| Legitimate Website | Fake Website      |
| ------------------ | ----------------- |
| `royalmail.co.uk`  | `royalmai1.co.uk` |

The attacker replaced the final letter **'l'** with the number **'1'**, making the fake domain look almost identical to the original.

**Hidden Links in HTML Emails:**

- HTML emails can display a legitimate-looking URL while redirecting users to a malicious website.
- Example: Displayed URL: `amazon.co.uk`
- Actual destination: `am4zon.co.uk`

**#How to check a suspicious link?**

<img width="778" height="318" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/1ca80eda-4351-499f-8a8d-07aff947a59a" />

Hover your mouse over the link in a desktop application to view its actual destination, usually displayed at the bottom of the screen.

**Note:** Spearphishing and Whaling can be extremely difficult to identify because attackers carefully customize messages for their targets. Even cybersecurity professionals can be fooled.

### **7. Staying Safe from Phishing Attacks**

1. **Delete Suspicious Emails:** Delete unknown or untrusted emails without opening them. Report suspicious emails as spam or forward them to the IT Security department.
2. **Avoid Unknown Attachments:** Never open attachments from untrusted emails, including unexpected attachments from legitimate contacts.
3. **Avoid Suspicious Links:** Never click embedded links unnecessarily. Visit the official website directly through your browser.
4. **Verify URLs:** If clicking a link is necessary, check that the domain is correct and leads to the expected destination.
5. **Update Devices:** Always keep your device and antivirus software up-to-date.
6. **Protect Personal Information:** Avoid making your email address and phone number publicly available.
7. **Use Burner Emails:** If publishing personal details is necessary, use a temporary email address created for that specific purpose and destroy it when no longer required.

### **8. What If You Fall for a Phishing Attack?**

- Don't panic.
- Immediately change the affected account's password.
- Contact IT Services if the attack happens at your workplace.
